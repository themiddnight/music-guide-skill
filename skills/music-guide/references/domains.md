# Domain prompts and limits

- **Theory:** Explain interval, scale, chord, voice leading, and harmonic rhythm in the requested context. SOLNA chords, MIDI and project notes, and score keys are stored intent. Per-track `pitchClassCounts` and the measured `audio.pitch_class_profile` show which pitch classes dominate, which helps with modal and pentatonic material; ask for listening evidence before asserting sounding harmony.
- **Scale checks:** When the user names a scale, state the pitch set you assume (spellings of scales such as Hirajoshi vary), then compute the out-of-scale share of each pitched track from `pitchClassCounts` and `pitchClassBeats`. Exclude percussion tracks (flagged `percussion`, or General MIDI channel 10 / channel index 9), whose note numbers are drum sounds. Use `audio.pitch_class_profile` only as supporting evidence, because drums, noise and harmonics spread energy across all pitch classes.
- **Composition:** Propose melody contour, motif development, phrase shape, and harmonic alternatives. Specify a short audition loop and what should improve.
- **Arrangement:** Discuss density, register, contrast, entrances, and transitions. `audio.short_term_loudness_timeline` shows where energy rises and falls; project markers and note summaries show stored sections and parts. Track names alone cannot prove what is audible.
- **Production:** Discuss sound choice, editing, groove, layering, and automation as proposals. MIDI beats are ticks divided by ticks per quarter note, not seconds without a tempo map.
- **Mixing:** Use band energy, spectral centroid, stereo correlation, side-to-mid ratio, crest factor and loudness range as whole-file context for tonal balance, width and dynamics. They cannot locate masking, a problem moment, or the track responsible; for attribution, request stems (each is measured separately) or solo-and-mute listening. Suggest A/B tests level-matched by integrated loudness.
- **Mastering:** Report integrated loudness (LUFS), loudness range (LU) and true peak (dBTP) from the BS.1770-4 measurements, and keep RMS and sample peak distinct from them. Compare with the user's target platform or reference, and recommend a dedicated meter for final delivery checks, especially for lossy-decoded or surround files.
- **Delivery targets:** Commonly published normalization references, to verify against each platform's current documentation before quoting: Spotify, YouTube, Tidal and Amazon Music about -14 LUFS integrated; Apple Music about -16 LUFS; true peak at or below -1 dBTP (lower for loud masters that will be lossy-encoded). Platforms turn louder masters down, so loudness above the target buys no level, only less dynamic range.
- **Mood and genre:** Treat labels as interpretations tied to user intent or audible evidence. Offer alternative descriptors and arrangement cues rather than asserting classifier output.
- **Artwork:** Propose palette, shape, typography, composition, and imagery based on a brief or clearly labeled interpretation; no artwork file is generated by the CLI.

- **Audio estimates:** Consult [audio estimates](audio-estimates.md) before interpreting inferred chords, rhythm, structure or melody. Distinguish beat-based analysis from fixed-window fallback. Use alternative suspended-chord interpretations when the root is unclear.
- **Energy and mood:** Energy, brightness and attack density describe signal features. `audio.mood_estimate` maps features to low-confidence mood labels using a stated heuristic, not a trained emotion model. Include alternatives and missing inputs. The user's interpretation takes precedence. Never infer a listener's emotional response from these scores.
